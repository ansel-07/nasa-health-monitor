## 5. SANS PROTOCOL — MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP

**Step 1 — Check for warning signs**

Let's check the warning signs first. In either eye, is there a new visual-field gap, central distortion, a need for correction beyond what is available, sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, or a severe headache with neurological signs?

**Step 2 — Notify the crew medical officer**

Time to bring in the crew medical officer. Notify them, then agree together on your check-ups and who's responsible for each one.

**Step 3 — Request priority medical review**

This finding deserves a closer look. Ask the crew medical officer for a priority medical review. Remember, a concerning number alone doesn't establish medical urgency.

**Step 4 — Review test results**

Let's put the pieces together. With your clinician, review corrected acuity and your personal baseline for each eye using Check-in results. Review OCT and visual fields when available. And remember, missing or invalid tests must never be treated as normal.

**Step 5 — Tests unavailable**

We shouldn't guess about missing results. Tell the crew medical officer which eye results are unavailable or invalid, and arrange an appropriate assessment. If OCT is missing, structural surveillance is incomplete.

**Step 6 — Follow-up guidance**

Stick to prescribed optical correction only, and follow the sleep and habitat procedures. Clearer focus doesn't mean the swelling has resolved. LBNP, thigh cuffs, supplements, and acetazolamide are research or clinician-only options, not automatic SANS treatments.

**Step 7 — Final: Follow-up**

Keep following the plan you've agreed on with your clinician. No drug has established rescue efficacy for SANS. Don't start medicines, pressure manipulation, or invasive eye procedures based on this app.

## 6. SANS PROTOCOL — EMERGENCY

**Step 1 — Stop activities**

Stop risky tasks and EVA now, and alert the crew medical officer immediately. Record when the symptoms began and which eye is affected. Don't wait for OCT.

**Step 2 — Local medical assessment**

Activate the on-board medical lead for an immediate ocular and neurological assessment. Don't assume every acute visual problem is SANS—the clinician decides the appropriate care and restrictions.

**Step 3 — Request assistance**

You need help now. Request immediate assistance from the crew medical officer, and don't continue the risky task.

**Step 4 — Check whether Earth support is required**

One thing to confirm: has the local clinician requested support from Earth while local care continues?

**Step 5 — Contact Earth from Mars**

Transmit the requested case summary to Earth and keep local care going under the pre-authorized protocol. Communication takes time out here, but it must never delay local assessment.

**Step 6 — Contact Earth from other environments**

Send the requested case summary to the flight surgeons while local medical care continues. Just to be clear, this app doesn't send messages or authorize transfers.

**Step 7 — Confirm clinical handover**

Before we mark this step as done, make sure a clinician has reviewed this episode and accepted responsibility for your ongoing care. Select Done only once that's confirmed. Otherwise, keep this task active and stay assisted.

**Step 8 — Final: Care transferred**

The clinical review and accepted handover are recorded. Keep following medical restrictions and your follow-up plan. This doesn't mean you're cured or cleared for EVA. Any transfer remains a decision for the clinical and mission teams.

## 7. MUSCLE ATROPHY PROTOCOL — MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP

**Step 1 — Check for warning signs**

Safety check first. Have you noticed sudden or rapidly worsening weakness, a new inability to move safely or complete egress, new intense muscle pain, breathing difficulty, or loss of consciousness? Remember, breathing difficulty or unconsciousness is a general emergency, not proof of muscle atrophy.

**Step 2 — Check limitations**

Let's get a clearer picture. Is this worsening unexpected, or is it stopping you from completing a task?

**Step 3 — Notify the crew medical officer**

Let's bring the crew medical officer up to speed. Tell them what's going on, and review your symptoms, any pain, your actual exercise load, and your strength trend together.

**Step 4 — Request priority medical review**

This deserves a closer look. Ask the crew medical officer for a priority review of the concerning finding or unexpected task limitation. Remember, a concerning number alone doesn't establish medical urgency.

**Step 5 — Review exercise and nutrition**

Let's take a look at your training and recovery. Review your resistive and aerobic exercise load, technique, and recovery with your exercise specialist instead of increasing the load yourself. Also, review your energy and protein intake against your approved nutrition plan.

**Step 6 — Final: Follow-up**

Keep logging your symptoms and training every day. Repeat comparable strength tests only as directed, and follow prescribed rehabilitation after return. Don't train through pain, add catch-up overload, or start supplements or medicines without medical advice. Steady and safe is the goal.

## 8. MUSCLE ATROPHY PROTOCOL — EMERGENCY

**Step 1 — Stop activities**

Stop exercising and any risky tasks immediately. Get into a safe position and alert the crew medical officer now. Don't repeat maximal tests or wait for another sensor reading.

**Step 2 — Local medical assessment**

Activate the on-board medical lead for immediate assessment and stabilization. The team needs to investigate possible injury or another acute cause. Follow the local emergency protocol without waiting for Earth.

**Step 3 — Request assistance**

You need help now. Request immediate assistance from the crew medical officer, and don't continue the risky task.

**Step 4 — Check whether Earth support is required**

One thing to confirm: has the local clinician requested support from Earth while local care continues?

**Step 5 — Contact Earth from ISS**

Send the summary requested by the clinician to the flight surgeons while local care continues. If the available care isn't sufficient, any decision about returning belongs to the medical and mission teams.

**Step 6 — Contact Earth from the Moon**

Send the clinician-requested summary to the flight surgeons while care continues in the habitat. Any transfer will depend on the available vehicle, resources, and clinical decision.

**Step 7 — Contact Earth from Mars**

Transmit the requested case summary to Earth and keep following the pre-authorized local protocol. Don't wait for an immediate reply from Earth. Local care comes first.

**Step 8 — Confirm clinical handover**

Before we mark this step as done, make sure a clinician has reviewed this episode and accepted responsibility for your ongoing care. Select Done only once that's confirmed. Otherwise, keep this task active and stay assisted.

**Step 9 — Final: Care transferred**

The clinical review and accepted handover are recorded. Keep following medical restrictions and diagnosis-specific rehabilitation. Any return or transfer depends on clinical need, the available vehicle, and resources—not on a sensor value alone.
